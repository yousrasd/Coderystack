I have zero users and I'm already rewriting my backend.

Sounds crazy, right?

I'm building Fisky in my free time. It's almost ready to launch, and this weekend I made a big decision:

**I'm moving from Firebase to Supabase.**

<Picture src="/post/post8-fisky-supabase/slide-2.jpeg" alt="The big decision: moving Fisky from Firebase to Supabase" width={800} height={800} />

## Why? Honestly, risk management.

Firebase has been great for getting started. The developer experience is amazing, especially for a solo developer moving fast.

But as I got closer to launch, I started thinking more about what could go wrong.

I've read many stories from developers on Reddit and other communities who woke up to unexpected cloud bills because of a bug, a misconfiguration, or unexpected usage.

Sometimes the problem isn't that your app succeeds.
Sometimes the problem is that something breaks silently and you don't notice until the bill arrives. A bug, a bad query pattern, or a sudden spike in activity could create a stressful situation.

<Picture src="/post/post8-fisky-supabase/slide-3.jpeg" alt="The silent risk: unexpected cloud bills" width={800} height={800} />

## I tried to protect myself first

I spent days improving my Firebase setup:

- tightening security rules
- validating data
- adding limits
- setting up budget alerts

Those things help.

But they don't completely remove the risk of usage-based costs growing unexpectedly.

<Picture src="/post/post8-fisky-supabase/slide-4.jpeg" alt="Hardening the Firebase setup: security rules, validation, limits, budget alerts" width={800} height={800} />

## Then I looked at Supabase

One thing that caught my attention was having a clearer spending boundary. With the spend cap option, I can choose to have my project stop when I reach my limit instead of letting costs continue increasing.

For a solo developer, that predictability matters.

<Picture src="/post/post8-fisky-supabase/slide-5.jpeg" alt="Supabase spend cap: a clear spending boundary" width={800} height={800} />

## Why not migrate later?

Could I migrate later after launch? Of course.

But migrating a backend with real users, production data, and paying customers is a very different challenge.

Migrating now, with zero users, is just moving boxes in an empty room.

So I'm choosing the painful thing early, while the cost of change is still low.

<Picture src="/post/post8-fisky-supabase/slide-6.jpeg" alt="Migrating with zero users is moving boxes in an empty room" width={800} height={800} />

## The lesson I'm learning as a solo developer

The best time to fix a risky technical decision is before it becomes an expensive one.

Building an app isn't only about shipping features. It's also about thinking ahead and reducing future problems before they happen.

<Picture src="/post/post8-fisky-supabase/slide-7.jpeg" alt="The lesson: fix risky technical decisions before they become expensive ones" width={800} height={800} />
